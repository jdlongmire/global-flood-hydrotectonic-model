## Baseline Model Statement

This model proposes that the pre-Flood Earth consisted of a supercontinent underlain by a fundamentally different crustal architecture than exists today: a globally integrated, high-connectivity hydraulic system capable of storing and transmitting vast volumes of liquid water through a channeled porous network spanning the crust and uppermost mantle.

In this regime, near-lithostatic pore pressures were sustained over continental scales, mechanically decoupling large crustal blocks and allowing fluid-assisted stability under normal conditions.

Evidence for the former existence of such large volumes of mobile water is preserved today in the substantial quantities of water stored in hydrous minerals such as wadsleyite and ringwoodite in the mantle transition zone; water incorporated into these minerals must have been chemically and physically available during their formation and therefore implies prior large-scale mobilization rather than permanent sequestration.

A catastrophic destabilization, plausibly initiated by an early-phase sequence of large meteor impacts, drove the system past a percolation and failure threshold, triggering rapid hydraulic depressurization, crustal collapse, and global surface inundation over an approximately year-long interval.

During this interval, the modern continental crustal layering, basin geometry, and much of the present stratigraphic record were produced through rapid erosion, transport, burial, and tectono-hydraulic reorganization.

Subsequent compaction, sealing, and mineral sequestration removed most of the original permeability and mobile water, yielding the mechanically compartmentalized crust observed today.

The model does not invoke new physical laws and is falsifiable by demonstrating that no physically plausible parameter regime exists in which a globally connected hydraulic crust could be maintained or catastrophically collapsed under known fluid, thermal, and mechanical constraints.

## Water Inventory Clarification

The model does not require all Flood water to exist simultaneously as a free surface ocean. It proposes that the pre-Flood Earth contained a larger total inventory of liquid water than is accessible today, partitioned between:

1. A more extensive global ocean
2. Large inland freshwater seas on the supercontinent
3. Large inland saltwater (marine) seas on the supercontinent
4. A highly connected porous substructure capable of storing water under pressure

During catastrophic failure, this water was rapidly redistributed, producing global inundation. Subsequent compaction, sealing, and mineral sequestration removed much of that water from surface circulation, leaving the smaller modern oceans and substantial internal reservoirs inferred today.

**Key point:** The water budget is a dynamic redistribution problem, not a static volume comparison.

## Pre-Flood World Model

The model envisions a pre-Flood supercontinent, broadly anticipating the outlines of today's continents, characterized by extensive inland freshwater and saltwater seas embedded within a globally integrated hydraulic crustal system. These basins provided large surface and subsurface water reservoirs prior to catastrophic failure.

During collapse, basin connectivity, salinity distribution, and pressure regimes were rapidly reorganized, producing widespread marine signatures far inland and contributing to the global inundation described in the Flood account. Subsequent burial, deformation, and mineral sequestration obscured this original hydrologic architecture, leaving only fragmentary evidence in the modern stratigraphic record.

### Why Inland Saltwater Seas Matter

| Evidence Type | Explanation Under This Model |
|---------------|------------------------------|
| Marine signatures far inland | Pre-existing marine basins, not post-Flood transgression |
| Mixed salinity indicators | Some basins marine, others freshwater prior to collapse |
| Marine organisms far from coasts | Ecological zonation in inland marine basins |
| Thick evaporites/carbonates inland | Restricted marine basins existed before catastrophe |
| Alternating marine-nonmarine sequences | Basin connectivity changes during collapse |

### What This Does NOT Claim

- Every inland basin was marine
- Salinity was uniform
- Modern basin locations map one-to-one onto pre-Flood basins

### What This DOES Claim

- The pre-Flood world was hydrologically complex
- Both marine and freshwater inland systems existed
- Later overrun, mixed, buried, and restructured during collapse

### Explanatory Advantage

Inland marine evidence is explained by **pre-existing basins plus catastrophic reorganization**, not by repeated transgressions over deep time. This is a fundamentally different explanatory framework.

## What the Model Claims

1. The heat problem in catastrophic plate tectonics is solved by friction collapse under near-lithostatic pore pressure (~20 W/m² vs ~600 W/m² conventional)

2. Sufficient energy (~10²⁵ J gravitational PE) was stored in elevated continental blocks to drive observed displacements

3. The mechanism has real-world analogs (submarine hydroplaning at 100s km scale)

4. A non-empty parameter regime exists where the mechanism operates

---

## What the Model Does Not Claim

1. Complete permeability evolution laws validated at crustal scale
2. Specific ark logistics or biogeography
3. Detailed fossil ordering mechanisms (qualitative only)
4. Exact cascade dynamics at seal failure
5. Post-Flood geological history in detail
6. Uniqueness (other mechanisms might also work)

---

## What Would Falsify the Model

### Category 1: Physical Impossibility

- F1.1: Demonstration that near-lithostatic pore pressure cannot be maintained at required scales under any parameter regime
- F1.2: Energy budget that cannot close (PE insufficient for observed displacement)
- F1.3: Heat generation that exceeds dissipation capacity under any friction model
- F1.4: Thermal/cavitation thresholds automatically crossed in all parameter regimes

### Category 2: Mechanism Failure

- F2.1: Proof that channeled-matrix porosity architecture cannot sustain supply/drainage balance
- F2.2: Permeability evolution that inevitably chokes or drains the system
- F2.3: Scale-up from analogs demonstrably fails at continental scale

### Category 3: Observational Inconsistency

- F3.1: Observations fundamentally inconsistent with any catastrophic depositional regime
- F3.2: Geological features requiring processes impossible under model constraints
