# Pressure-Entrapment Logic Chain

**Date:** 2025-12-18
**Status:** Defense Material

---

## Core Argument

If Earth underwent rapid, system-scale restructuring, then extreme hydraulic pressures are not speculative - they are **required by the physics** of confined fluid flow and deformation.

Those pressures provide a natural mechanism both for global inundation and for subsequent mineral sequestration of water, consistent with the large internal reservoirs inferred today.

---

## 1. Short Timescale Restructuring Implies Extreme Pressures

If large volumes of water are mobilized rapidly through a connected crustal system, pressure is set by:

- Overburden stress
- Confinement
- Connectivity
- Rate of release

**You don't get to choose whether pressures are large.** They are imposed by geometry and timescale.

Near-lithostatic pore pressures are not exotic. They are a **natural outcome** when:

- Permeability is high
- Drainage is restricted
- Deformation is rapid

This is already observed today in:

| System | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| Hydrofracturing | Pressure-driven fracture propagation |
| Fault-valving | Episodic pressure release on fault zones |
| Subduction dehydration | Pulsed water release from descending slabs |
| Overpressured basins | Trapped fluids at near-lithostatic conditions |

The model is just scaling the regime.

---

## 2. Connection to Mineral Water Entrapment

This is the connection many critics miss.

To entrap large volumes of water into:
- Wadsleyite
- Ringwoodite
- Other hydrous phases

You need:
- **High pressure**
- **Water availability**
- **Rapid phase transitions**

A slow, diffuse trickle over billions of years is one pathway.

A **short, violent, high-pressure reorganization** is another.

**Both rely on the same physics: pressure-driven incorporation.**

When someone objects:
> "That water is bound, not liquid"

The response:
> Bound *now* because pressure and phase conditions forced it there *then*.

The bound state is evidence of prior mobilization, not evidence against it.

---

## 3. Deep-Time Model Uses the Same Physics

Here is the irony being exposed.

Deep-time geology already assumes:

- Slabs carrying water to 410-660 km depth
- Pressure-driven mineral transitions
- Large-scale water incorporation
- Long-term retention under mantle conditions

**All of that requires immense pressures.**

The only real disagreement:

| Framework | Pressure Application |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Deep time | Slowly and diffusely over billions of years |
| This model | Rapidly and catastrophically over ~1 year |

Invoking high hydraulic pressure is not special pleading. It's using the **same physics with different boundary conditions**.

---

## 4. Why "Not Enough Water" Collapses Under This Framing

Once you allow:

- Pre-collapse high connectivity
- Near-lithostatic pore pressures
- Rapid mobilization

The objection:
> "There isn't enough water to cover the continents"

becomes badly mis-posed.

Because:

- The water does not need to exist as a static surface ocean
- It can be **stored under pressure**
- **Released episodically**
- And later **sequestered into minerals**

The water budget is a **dynamic problem**, not a bucket-counting exercise.

Static volume comparisons miss the physics entirely.

---

## 5. The Logic Chain

```
Massive, rapid restructuring
         ↓
Extreme pressures (unavoidable by physics)
         ↓
Large-scale water mobilization
         ↓
Mobilization + pressure → mineral entrapment
         ↓
Mineral entrapment today → evidence of that past regime
```

We are not adding assumptions. We are **following the consequences of the timescale**.
