# Issue: Geomagnetic Implications of Hydrotectonic Collapse

**Status:** Open
**Priority:** Low
**Category:** Physics / Predictions
**Phase:** 6 (Observable Predictions)
**Confidence:** MED-LOW (plausible pathways exist; evidence is indirect and contested)

---

## Description

Explore whether extreme global-scale hydrotectonic activity during Stage 2 could plausibly result in multiple geomagnetic field shifts or excursions, without requiring full dynamo shutdown or polarity reversal in the modern sense.

**Note:** This is one of the most speculative components of the model and needs careful scoping.

---

## Coupling Pathways (Physically Orthodox)

### A. Core-Mantle Boundary (CMB) Torque Coupling
- Large, rapid mass redistribution changes moment of inertia
- Alters angular momentum exchange with core
- Can perturb core flow patterns
- Hydrotectonic collapse involves continent-scale mass motion over short timescales

### B. Heat Flux Heterogeneity at CMB
- Geodynamo is sensitive to spatial and temporal variability in heat flux
- Rapid tectonic reorganization alters mantle convection patterns
- Changes thermal boundary conditions at CMB
- Secular models accept that changes in lower mantle heat flux can trigger excursions

### C. Electromagnetic Induction from Massive Fluid Flow
- Conductive saline water moving at high velocity through lithosphere
- Normally negligible, potentially non-negligible at:
  - Global scale
  - Sustained high-pressure flow
  - Kilometer-scale conductive pathways
- Could distort, weaken, or locally reorganize field lines

---

## Key Distinction

**Magnetic excursions:** Rapid intensity drops, directional instability, partial flips
**Polarity reversals:** Complete dynamo reorganization

The claim only requires **excursions or rapid directional shifts**, not stable long-term reversals.

---

## Why Flood-Year Conditions Are Uniquely Suited

The model stacks three destabilizers **simultaneously** that normally occur separately and slowly:

1. Rapid global mass redistribution
2. Extreme fluid motion and pressure collapse
3. Abrupt thermal boundary changes

Dynamo theory is nonlinear. Stacked perturbations can push across instability thresholds that slow forcing cannot.

---

## Vulnerabilities (Must Be Acknowledged)

1. **Timescale compression** - Many paleomagnetic interpretations assume thousands of years per transition; model compresses to months

2. **Core response time** - Debate over how fast core flow can reorganize; estimates range from decades to much faster for excursions

3. **Overprinting ambiguity** - Rapid depositional environments complicate remanence acquisition; critics will argue signal mixing

## Predicted Signatures

- Compressed paleomagnetic sequences
- Abrupt directional variability
- Intensity collapse rather than prolonged transitional states
- Multiple excursions within stratigraphic flood interval

## Survivable Paragraph (Draft)

> The extreme hydrotectonic activity proposed for Stage 2 entails rapid global mass redistribution, transient thermal boundary changes at the core-mantle interface, and sustained large-scale conductive fluid motion. Each of these processes is independently known to influence geomagnetic field behavior. Acting concurrently, they provide a plausible mechanism for repeated geomagnetic excursions or rapid directional instabilities during the flood interval, without requiring long-term dynamo shutdown or stable polarity reversals. This interpretation predicts compressed paleomagnetic sequences characterized by abrupt directional variability and intensity collapse rather than prolonged transitional states.
