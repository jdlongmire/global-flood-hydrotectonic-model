# Issue: Impact Dampening in Saturated Porous Crust

**Status:** Open
**Priority:** Medium
**Category:** Physics / Trigger Mechanism
**Phase:** 4 (Thermal/Physical Stability)

---

## Description

Explore how meteor impacts would behave differently in a water-saturated, porous pre-Flood crust compared to dry/consolidated post-Flood crust.

**Core hypothesis:** Impacts on saturated porous crust would be dampened at the surface but cause extensive subsurface damage.

---

## Physical Reasoning

### Surface Effects (Dampened)
- Pore fluid compression absorbs impact energy
- Plastic deformation of saturated matrix vs. brittle fracture
- Energy partitioned into fluid expulsion rather than cratering
- Result: Muted surface cratering, less obvious surface evidence

### Subsurface Effects (Enhanced)
- Energy transmitted as hydraulic shock through connected pore network
- Extensive fracturing of aquitards and seal layers
- Pressure cascades propagating through sealed compartments
- Result: Distributed seal failure, Stage 2 trigger mechanism enhanced

---

## Implications

1. **Trigger efficiency** - Impacts become more efficient at initiating subsurface seal failure than at creating surface craters

2. **Evidence asymmetry** - Triggering impacts may leave little surface record while their subsurface effects dominate the geological signature

3. **Crater record** - May explain absence of obvious impact signatures at Flood boundary - energy went into hydraulic damage, not cratering

4. **Impact morphology** - Pre-Flood impacts would produce different crater forms than post-Flood impacts on dried/consolidated crust
